- Bring Your Own Agent (BYOA) is now stable in Android Studio Rabbit 2, running over 40 coding agents that speak the open Agent Client Protocol (ACP) inside the IDE.

- A machine gate in code sends PRs touching auth, authorization, personal data, production DB migrations, payments, infrastructure or CI settings to people and keeps generated files from the LLM.
- The LLM returns JSON with `businessLogicChanged` and its reason, `confidence` and severity-tagged `risks`, asking whether production behaviour changes and, if so, whether the change is simple and routine.
- The system instruction fixes the trust boundary, the fallback to people and the schema, criteria precede the diff fence, text after it counts as diff, and injection attempts are reported as risks.
- Core areas such as dispatch and payments and PRs over 1,000 lines are judged three times and merged by OR, as people commented on 13–27% of PRs under 200 lines but on 60% of those over 1,000.
- A pure Go function approves only if every gate passes and confidence reaches 0.80, or 0.95 in core areas, plus 0.10 for authors who do not own the code, and hands the verdict to CI as an exit code.
- Diffs over 512 KB, binaries and failed LLM calls go to people, and the check runs on `pull_request_target` with main's code and the diff from the GitHub API, never executing the PR's code.
- In about two months, approved PRs caused post-release defects at about half the needs_human rate, and LLM-flagged PRs drew review findings at over 2x the approved rate, unlike business-logic stops.
- Most missed defects needed code outside the diff, and when Gemini 2.5 Flash reached end of service, re-judging past PRs on the same model matched production verdicts only about 85% of the time.
- A `needs_human` label feeds dashboards of PRs sent to people yet merged with no findings, and can drive `STOP_IF` rules such as Addy Osmani's factory pausing once more than 3 items await review.
